How the one rep max calculator works

Your one rep max (1RM) is the heaviest weight you could lift for a single clean repetition. Testing it directly is tiring and carries injury risk, so most lifters estimate it from a heavy set of several reps. This calculator runs your set through four well-known formulas and shows the average, which smooths out the quirks of any single equation.

The formulas

  • Epley: 1RM = weight × (1 + reps / 30)
  • Brzycki: 1RM = weight × 36 / (37 − reps)
  • Lombardi: 1RM = weight × reps0.10
  • O'Conner: 1RM = weight × (1 + 0.025 × reps)

Epley and Brzycki are the most widely used. They agree closely for sets of 2 to 8 reps and drift apart as reps climb, which is why the estimate is best from a heavy, low-rep set.

Tips for an accurate estimate

  • Use a set taken close to failure, with good form and a full range of motion.
  • Stay between 2 and 8 reps. Accuracy drops noticeably above 10.
  • Treat the result as a starting point for programming, and adjust based on how the weights actually feel.
  • Expect different numbers for different lifts. Your squat, bench and deadlift 1RMs are separate.

Using your 1RM in training

The percentage table above turns your estimate into working weights. Many strength programs prescribe sets at 70 to 85% of 1RM for building strength, with lighter percentages for higher-rep volume work. The rep counts shown are rough averages, and individuals vary quite a bit, especially at higher percentages.
